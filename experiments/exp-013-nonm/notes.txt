  legal_all_rows      584.9   trained on every departure with no _flt column, scored on the segment

  oracle_block_sched  211.2   the same model plus the TRUE BLOCK-SCHED as a feature
  oracle_arithmetic     0.0   (MVT-SCHED) - (BLOCK-SCHED), the identity
  segment_mean        692.0   predict the training segment mean
Two things fall out.
(1) legal_all_rows 584.9 already beats the best baseline (prc.tail_model v2, 651.4) on july, and
    segment-only training is far worse (753.0), confirming tail_model's finding 11 on this
    segment rather than inheriting it.
(2) The 211.2 vs 0.0 gap is the whole finding. Given a feature that determines the target
    exactly, the tree still loses 211 s, because the target is the difference of two quantities
    of scale ~4600 s and a tree can only approximate a subtraction with splits. That is a
    representation penalty, not an information one, and it applies to EVERY candidate in
    question 1, since all of them are differences of large timestamps. It is direct evidence for
    question 3: model BLOCK-SCHED as the target and subtract, rather than predicting taxi from
    two large terms and hoping the tree finds the difference.
july_loo, first three configs (segment RMSE / all-rows RMSE):
  base    570.8 / 518.0
  op      572.9 / 516.1
  stand   652.1 / 498.4
The stand family is the sharpest result so far and it splits the two populations: it IMPROVES
all rows by 19.6 s and DEGRADES the segment by 81.3 s. Its coverage is 96 %+ on NM rows and
21.9 % on the segment (the stand bounds need a stand neighbour, and these delayed flights often
have none), so a model trained on every departure learns to lean on a feature the segment does
not have, and the segment's rows land in leaves shaped by rows that do. That is a concrete
mechanism for why "train on all departures and score a sub-population" can go wrong, and it
argues for either coverage-matched features or a head trained where coverage matches.
BUG in my own bracket feature, found because its result contradicted the coverage story
(99 % coverage but +102 s on the segment). `brk_next_span` used searchsorted(..., "left") on
both sides, so for any departure that has an AOBT — 98 % of training rows — the "next
neighbour" was the row itself and the column was literally its own take-off minus AOBT.
Verified: 100.0 % of AOBT rows had brk_next_gap == 0 and brk_next_span == own span, against
0.1 % of segment rows. Not label leakage (AOBT is a legal column) but a train/serve semantic
mismatch: one column meaning two different things in the two populations, so the model learned
"this is my own span" from the majority and applied it to the segment where it is a neighbour's.
Fixed with "right" on the next side; residual self-reads 0.99 % (genuine same-second
neighbours), segment coverage unchanged at 98.9 %. Cache, ablation.json and OOF cleared and the
whole ablation restarted rather than patching the affected configs, so no result in the table
predates the fix.
