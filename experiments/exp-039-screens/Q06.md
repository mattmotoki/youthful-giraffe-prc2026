# Q06 — the census of recoverable missing joins: **NEGATIVE** on recovery, matcher kept

**Verdict: negative.** The joins are recoverable and the matcher is accurate — 99.87 % accepted-ID
agreement on 1,550 accepted rows, 0.01 % wrong acceptance when the correct donor is removed — but
the recovered anchor is not usable as a prediction. On the naturally unmatched `nonm` fold rows the
donor anchor scores RMSE 2,324 / 662 / 9,302 s against v10's 475 / 374 / 435 s, `A/B` is
−0.006 / −0.112 / −0.010, and substituting it costs **32.44 s of the primary board**. The census also
answers the queue's first question directly: after the clock windows there is **not one** query with
more than one distinct donor id, so the ambiguity the queue worried about is not what limits this.

**The three quantities the queue asks to separate, separated:** donor existence 15.16 %, matching
accuracy 99.87 %, predictive benefit **negative**.

Everything below is `code/q06.py` and `code/donor.py` on the fold-side v10 of `code/v10.py`,
measured through `code/packet.py` (`analysis/q06.json`, `analysis/packet-q06.json`, log
`logs/q06.log`, 7.5 s at 4 threads). Section 8's tables are generated from that record by
`code/tables.py`.

**Defect found and fixed before the review.** `donor.py` converted `timestamp[us]` columns with
`astype("int64") / 1e9`, making every clock 1,000× too small and both donor windows effectively
unbounded. It was caught by inspecting one KL1818 example whose gap printed as 190.341 instead of
190,341. `epoch()` now reads `s.dtype.unit`; the repaired conversion matches direct timestamp
subtraction with max error 0. The first run's 20.55 % accepted-ID agreement was that defect; the
repaired figure is 99.87 %.

**Round 2 repairs.** Sol's round 2 accepted Q06's substance as NON-BLOCKING and named two record
repairs, both made. (a) The donor-anchor statistic now carries the same 1/2/3-day moving-block
sensitivity every other screen reports (T4, last column); on july_loo the 3-day interval is
[−10,658,822, −1,713,173] s², so the negative is not a one-day artifact. (b) The predeclared
20,000-row sample now draws from its own seeded generator rather than the shared one, so an
unrelated bootstrap earlier in the run cannot move it. That change re-drew the **gold-removed
pricing** sample, and the swing it produced is itself worth recording: the wrong-acceptance RATE is
stable at 0.01 %, but the per-row price on 1 to 5 accepted rows is not — across two draws july_loo's
wrong-anchor RMSE read 15,166 s and then 10,187 s, and january's read 1,026 s and then 123 s. §2
now quotes the rate, not the price.

**Round 1 repairs.** Sol's round 1 found three more. (10) The report called 7.74 % "candidate
recall"; that is recall over all 20,000 queries, most of which have no counterpart in the file at
all. The correct denominator is the 3,033 queries whose gold id exists: **recall is 51.04 %**, not
7.74 %. (11) Reciprocal uniqueness was computed per donor row rather than per accepted `FLIGHT_ID`,
so one flight appearing twice looked like a conflict; it is now `nunique` over the accepted id.
(12) The january donor-anchor SSE share was labelled "clean" but computed all-row: the clean share is
**0.189 %**, the all-row share 0.079 %. All three are corrected here and in the ledger.

## 1. The census on the ranking file (T1)

388 `noac` rows (`AIRCRAFT_TYPE_mvt` null) and 4,895 `nonm` rows (no NM join). Accepted: **43
(11.08 %)** and **206 (4.21 %)**.

The stage-by-stage table is the interesting part. Before the clock windows, 292 noac and 3,581 nonm
queries have more than one distinct candidate id. After the windows, **zero** do. The windows —
arrival within [0, 12 h], duplicate within ±60 s — are not a tie-breaker over an ambiguous set; they
are the constraint that makes the set a singleton or empty. 316 noac and 4,468 nonm queries are
removed by a clock conflict, which is why coverage is 11.08 % and 4.21 % rather than the 83.76 % and 91.83 % of queries that have SOME same-key, same-leg donor row.

Coverage is bounded by destination, not by the matcher: only 18.30 % of noac and 14.71 % of nonm
rows have a destination among the ten airports, and among those the acceptance rates are 60.56 % and
28.61 %. A donor can only exist if the counterpart movement is in the file.

## 2. Matching accuracy, on masked-joined rows (T2)

20,000 rows sampled at seed 39 from rows that DO have a join, with their join hidden, so the gold
`FLIGHT_ID_mvt` is known. Four different denominators, kept apart:

- **15.16 %** (3,033) have a known counterpart anywhere in the file.
- **51.04 %** (1,548 of those 3,033) keep the gold id inside the clock windows — **candidate
  recall**.
- **7.75 %** (1,550 of 20,000) are accepted.
- **99.87 %** (1,548 of 1,550) of accepted ids are the gold id; 1,550 distinct ids on 1,550
  acceptances.

The gap between 51 % recall and 99.87 % precision is the design: the windows throw away half of the
recoverable donors to keep the accepted set almost exactly right.

**The stress (T3).** Removing every row that carries a query's own gold id — 23,033 rows — leaves
2 acceptances out of 20,000, a **0.01 %** wrong-acceptance rate. That rate is the stable quantity
and it is what the screen relies on. Priced on labelled fold rows, the few wrong acceptances are
catastrophic per row — the recorded draw gives anchor RMSE 10,186.6 / 14,612.3 / 123.0 s against v10's
2,664.7 / 188.5 / 583.6 s — but 1 to 5 rows per fold cannot support a stable price, and a different draw
of the same predeclared sample moved july_loo's figure by a third and january's by an order of
magnitude. Read T3 as "wrong acceptances are rare, and the ones that happen are not small", not as a
per-row cost estimate.

## 3. The donor anchor is not a prediction (T4, T5)

On the naturally unmatched `nonm` fold rows — 3,595 / 1,554 / 1,369, of which 195 / 65 / 73 are
accepted with a usable `AOBT_3_flt` — the donor anchor scores RMSE 2,324.3 / 661.5 / 9,301.8 against
v10's 474.9 / 373.8 / 435.2, with `A/B` −0.0056 / −0.1124 / −0.0102 on rows carrying
0.286 / 0.131 / 0.189 % of each fold's clean SSE (T4).

`A/B` is negative on every fold: the donor anchor is not merely mis-scaled, it points the wrong way.
Clipping the anchor to a plausible range moves january's `A/B` to +0.3303 and its RMSE to a fraction
of 9,302, which identifies the problem as a **tail**, not a bias.

The percentiles (T5) make that explicit. On july_loo the donor's median absolute error is 182.0 s
against v10's 183.7 s — the median donor is as good as v10 — and the donor is closer on 46.7 % of
accepted rows. At the 99th percentile the donor is 11,440 s against v10's 2,534 s; on january it is
35,608 s against 1,287 s. A handful of accepted donors whose off-block report belongs to a different
turnaround destroys the arm.

Substituting the anchor on every accepted row moves the primary all-row board from **309.0772 to
341.5210**, a loss of **32.44 s** (T6). The 1/2/3-day moving-block intervals on the same statistic
never approach zero (july_loo 3-day [−10,658,822, −1,713,173] s²), so the negative does not rest on
one bad day.

## 4. Support and ambiguity

- **The negative is about the anchor, not the match.** The matcher's own accuracy is measured
  separately, on a different population, and it is high. The donor's `AOBT_3_flt` is a real off-block
  report for a real counterpart flight; it simply does not determine the departure's own off-block
  time closely enough for a 250-second-scale problem.
- **The clipped result is the honest MAYBE inside the negative,** and it is small: even at
  `A/B = +0.33` on january, the affected population is 73 rows carrying 0.189 % of the fold's clean
  SSE. A perfect anchor on those rows cannot move the board by a tenth of a second.
- **Coverage is destination-bound.** Raising coverage means matching to destinations outside the ten
  airports, which the file does not contain. No matcher change reaches those rows.
- **Errors are priced on labelled rows only.** The ranking-file census and the labelled pricing are
  separate populations throughout, and no ranking-file quantity is used as a fitting signal (score-feedback rule).
- **Selection.** Window widths, the key normalisation (uppercase and strip only), the sample seed
  and the acceptance rule are predeclared in `analysis/q06.json` under `predeclared`. The clip range
  is a diagnostic, reported as one, and no arm is served through it.

## 5. Current-predictor benefit

**Negative: −32.44 s on the primary board.** There is no version of the substitution that helps.

## 6. Next repair or fit

1. **Keep the matcher as an instrument, not as a predictor.** It is accurate enough to answer
   questions about which rows share an airframe or a turnaround; that is worth having in the record
   for a future feature (previous-leg identity), and it costs nothing to keep.
2. **The one repair that could revive an anchor** is a robust combiner rather than a substitution:
   use the donor anchor only where a second clock corroborates it, and never as the sole anchor.
   That is a member-fit-shaped change and is out of this screen's scope; it goes to the backlog with
   the revival condition "median-competitive donor anchor with a tail guard beats v10 on the
   accepted rows".
3. **Do not widen the windows.** Widening buys recall against the 51.04 %, but the stress shows the
   cost of a wrong acceptance is up to 213,485,212 s² per row on the draw recorded here; at 0.01 % wrong acceptance the current
   windows are already at the point where precision is what protects the board.
